# Zen deck: illustration descriptions for the major cards

Prompts for an image model, one per major card. The card list follows `docs/zen-buddhism-research.md`. Each prompt is self-contained and can be pasted as it stands; the shared style block below is already folded into every prompt.

## Shared visual style

- **Medium:** Japanese ink-wash painting (sumi-e) in the Muromachi manner associated with Shūbun and Sesshū. Black and grey ink on warm, slightly textured cream paper, with dry-brush strokes and unpainted space.
- **Colour:** ink only, plus one accent of muted vermilion per card (a seal, a robe, a flower, a flame). No other colour.
- **Format:** portrait, 2:3 aspect ratio, with a narrow plain border of untouched paper.
- **Frames by strand:**
  - Card 0 and The Way (I–X): the scene sits inside a single hand-brushed ensō circle, following the round format of the traditional ox-herding pictures. The circle is the same weight and size on every card.
  - The Transmission (XI–XVII): the scene fills a tall rectangle, like a hanging scroll, with no circle.
- **Figures:** the herdsman on cards I–X is the same person throughout: a young man in a plain short robe and straw sandals, bareheaded. The ox is a dark water ox and keeps the same colour on every card; it does not whiten, following Kuoan rather than Puming.
- **Text:** none. No titles, numbers or legible characters, since image models render them badly; titles are added in layout. A small vermilion seal in the lower corner carries only an abstract mark.
- **Avoid:** photorealism, 3D rendering, anime or manga styling, gold leaf, glowing or haloed light effects, lotus-and-incense New Age imagery, Western tarot borders.

Style suffix used at the end of every prompt:

> Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

## Cards

### 0 — Mu (無)

**Prompt.** A single large hand-brushed ink circle (ensō) fills the card, and the scene sits inside it. At the centre stands an open wooden gateway of two posts and a crossbeam, with no door and no wall, so the empty space between the posts is the focus. In front of it a young bareheaded monk in a plain short robe and straw sandals bows slightly toward an old robed master seated on a flat stone, who holds one palm up. A small dark dog sits between them, looking up. A pine branch hangs at upper left. The master's robe is the only muted vermilion. Still mood. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: open gateway with no door; monk; old master (Zhaozhou); one dog; empty space between the posts.
- Composition: gate centred in the circle, figures in the lower third, upper half left mostly empty. Eye line runs from the monk through the dog to the master and then up into the gateway.
- Continuity: the monk is drawn as the young herdsman of cards I-X (same face, robe, sandals), so the sequence starts with him before the search. No ox appears.
- Watch for: legible or pseudo-characters on the gate beam or posts; a door or closed gate; a second dog; a puppy drawn as cute or anime; the master given a halo; vermilion spreading to more than the robe.

### I — Searching for the Ox (尋牛)

**Prompt.** Inside a single hand-brushed ink circle (ensō), a young bareheaded herdsman in a plain short robe and straw sandals stands in tall wild grass on a wide empty plain, one hand shading his eyes, the other holding a coiled rope. He looks out and slightly to the left. Maple trees stand at the right edge, and distant mountains fade into pale grey wash behind him. A stream winds across the middle ground. There is no ox anywhere in the scene. The coiled rope is a muted vermilion. Wide, open, lonely mood, autumn. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: lone herdsman; rope in hand; tall grass; maples; distant mountains; stream; no ox.
- Composition: figure small, left of centre and slightly below the middle, looking toward the larger empty area at right.
- Continuity: first appearance of the herdsman as a young man in the ensō; the ox is not yet visible. Next card shows only its tracks.
- Watch for: an ox hidden in the background or a cow-shaped rock; a second person; a staff or hat added; a cluttered foreground.

### II — Seeing the Traces (見跡)

**Prompt.** Inside a single hand-brushed ink circle (ensō), the young bareheaded herdsman in a plain short robe and straw sandals crouches at the edge of a stream and bends low to look at a line of large cloven hoof prints pressed into soft mud and grass. He holds his coiled rope loosely in one hand. A few trees with thick trunks stand behind him, and the prints continue along the bank toward the right and disappear into tall grass. There is no ox in the scene. His rope is a muted vermilion. Quiet, attentive mood, early autumn. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: herdsman stooping; hoof prints in a clear row; stream; trees; no ox.
- Composition: prints form a diagonal from lower left toward the upper right, leading the eye out of the frame into grass. Figure at left.
- Continuity: same herdsman, now close to the ground after standing and scanning in card I. The ox is still absent but its path is shown.
- Watch for: dog or human paw prints; round prints instead of cloven ones; an ox in the grass; modern footwear marks.

### III — Seeing the Ox (見牛)

**Prompt.** Inside a single hand-brushed ink circle (ensō), the young bareheaded herdsman in a plain short robe and straw sandals stands at the left, one hand raised in surprise, looking across a small stream. On the far bank, half hidden behind the trunks of willow trees, a large dark water ox shows its hindquarters, back and tail, its head turned away and out of sight. A bird sits on a willow branch above. Soft spring light, thin ground mist. The herdsman's rope, held at his side, is a muted vermilion. Hushed, wondering mood. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: herdsman; ox seen partly, from behind or the side; willows; bird; water between them.
- Composition: the two are on opposite sides of the circle with the stream and mist between, and unpainted space in the centre.
- Continuity: the ox appears here for the first time, dark, and keeps this colouring on every later card. The herdsman holds the same rope.
- Watch for: a white or brown ox; a cow with an udder; horns that are straight or too long; ox facing the viewer; a second animal.

### IV — Catching the Ox (得牛)

**Prompt.** Inside a single hand-brushed ink circle (ensō), the young bareheaded herdsman in a plain short robe and straw sandals leans back with both hands on a long rope looped around the neck of a large dark water ox. The ox pulls forward with lowered head and curved horns, hooves dug into rocky ground. The herdsman's feet are planted wide and his body slants against the pull. A windswept tree and low cloud hang behind them, and the rope is drawn taut between them. The rope is a muted vermilion. Tense, strained mood. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: taut rope between man and ox; ox resisting; herdsman braced; rough ground; the ox is male and dark.
- Composition: strong diagonal, ox at the right pulling out of the circle, man at the left pulling in. Keep the upper third light.
- Continuity: first physical contact. The ox stays the same dark colour as in III; the rope is the one carried since I.
- Watch for: the rope drawn slack or tied to nothing; a rodeo or bullfight pose; bull anatomy exaggerated; a whip in place of the rope; the ox turned white.

### V — Herding the Ox (牧牛)

**Prompt.** Inside a single hand-brushed ink circle (ensō), the young bareheaded herdsman in a plain short robe and straw sandals walks at the left of a large dark water ox, holding a short rope tied to a ring in its nose with one hand and a thin whip in the other, carried low and unused. The ox walks calmly beside him with its head level and its horns curved back, along a grassy path under a large pine. A few strokes of grass and one distant hill. The rope is a muted vermilion. Calm, steady mood. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: man walking, ox walking beside him; nose rope; whip; tree; grass.
- Composition: horizontal movement left to right across the lower middle, with a large calm area above.
- Continuity: same rope as IV, now slack. The whip is first seen here and is put down in VII. Ox is calm and still dark.
- Watch for: rope tied to the horns instead of the nose; whip raised to strike; the man riding; a cow shape; a second herd animal.

### VI — Riding the Ox Home (騎牛歸家)

**Prompt.** Inside a single hand-brushed ink circle (ensō), the young bareheaded herdsman in a plain short robe and straw sandals sits sideways on the back of a large dark water ox, playing a short bamboo flute. The ox walks slowly to the right with its head low and the rope hanging loose. Soft evening mist lies between low hills, and a bent willow leans over the path. A pale round moon sits low in the sky. The flute is a muted vermilion. Peaceful, easy mood. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: man seated on the ox; transverse bamboo flute; ox walking; evening mist; loose rope.
- Composition: rider slightly above centre, moving toward the right edge; mist as unpainted band.
- Continuity: the ox and man now move together as in V but without the whip, which is dropped. The ox is still dark. Next card has no ox.
- Watch for: a Western flute or a horn; the man facing backward without reason; a saddle; a child instead of the young man; the moon painted bright or glowing.

### VII — The Ox Forgotten, the Man Alone (忘牛存人)

**Prompt.** Inside a single hand-brushed ink circle (ensō), the young bareheaded herdsman in a plain short robe sits alone on a mat outside a small thatched hut, eyes lightly closed, hands resting in his lap. A whip and a coil of rope lie on the ground beside him, put aside. There is no ox. A large pale moon stands in the sky above the hut, a few reeds grow at the edge of the ground, and distant hills are faint. The rope is a muted vermilion. Still, restful mood. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: man alone; thatched hut; whip and rope laid down; no ox; high moon.
- Composition: figure and hut left of centre, large empty sky and moon at right.
- Continuity: the whip and rope from V and VI are set down here, and the ox does not appear again. The hut is used again in IX.
- Watch for: an ox in the doorway or as a shadow; the moon drawn as a bright disc with a halo; a roof of tile instead of thatch; meditation poses with lotus imagery.

### VIII — Ox and Man Both Forgotten (人牛俱忘)

**Prompt.** Inside a single hand-brushed ink circle (ensō), an empty clearing at the edge of a field: open, level ground with sparse low tufts of grass and a single small pebble, a faint line of distant hills in pale grey wash with a band of mist below them, and three thin trees with a few bare branches standing together at the right side. No person, no animal, no hut and no path. Most of the circle is open ground and unpainted paper. The only muted vermilion is a small square seal outside the circle at lower right. Quiet, vacant mood. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: the open clearing; sparse ground cover; a few trees at one side; no herdsman and no ox.
- Composition: horizon a little above the middle of the circle, trees clustered at the right, the centre left open. The clearing should read as the place where the figures were, not as a new scene.
- Continuity: the herdsman and ox of I–VII are absent. The traditional picture is an empty circle; this deck shows an empty clearing instead, by design decision (2026-10-10), so the card does not read as a missing image.
- Watch for: a figure, animal, hoofprints or rope added to the scene; a dense forest; a path leading somewhere; a second red element.

### IX — Returning to the Source (返本還源)

**Prompt.** Inside a single hand-brushed ink circle (ensō), a blossoming plum tree grows on a rocky bank at the left, its branches loaded with small red flowers, and a clear stream flows past beneath it from upper right to lower left. A few rocks sit in the water. A small thatched hut is just visible among bamboo at the far side, and distant mountains lie in soft grey wash. No person and no animal appears. The blossoms are the muted vermilion. Peaceful spring mood, quiet landscape. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: flowering tree; flowing stream; rocks; no figures. A hut in the distance is optional and should be small.
- Composition: tree on the left as a vertical, stream as a diagonal, open water and space on the right.
- Continuity: the landscape of I and II returns, now without searching or traces. The herdsman does not appear, and the ox does not appear.
- Watch for: a figure on the bank; a fisherman or boat; blossoms in many colours; cherry petals falling in a stylised swirl; a tree drawn as a decorative gold-screen motif.

### X — Entering the Market with Open Hands (入鄽垂手)

**Prompt.** Inside a single hand-brushed ink circle (ensō), the herdsman, now older, stands barefoot on a dirt path at the edge of a village, bareheaded, with a round bare belly showing through an open ragged robe. He laughs broadly. A cloth sack hangs over one shoulder, a wine gourd hangs from a staff in his hand, and his other hand is held out open, palm up, toward a young traveller with a coiled rope at the right. Simple roofs and a market awning are sketched behind him, with a bare tree that has begun to flower. His sack is a muted vermilion. Warm, cheerful mood. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: barefoot laughing figure with open robe and belly; sack; gourd; staff; open hand; market or village edge; no ox.
- Composition: figure large and slightly left of centre, the young traveller smaller at right, village sketched in light grey at the back.
- Continuity: the same herdsman as I-VII, aged and heavier, in the manner of the laughing monk Budai. The ox is not shown. The bare tree about to flower links to the blossoming tree of IX, The young traveller with a rope echoes the searcher of card I, which closes the cycle; whether the traditional picture's second figure is a new seeker is a reading, not something the Kuoan text states.
- Watch for: a gold Buddha statue look, a shaved head with a halo or lotus seat; a crowd or a busy market; legible shop signs; the sack drawn in black rather than vermilion; the ox returning as a toy or a companion.

### XI — The Flower and the Smile

**Prompt.** A tall hanging-scroll composition on cream paper. On a rocky ledge at upper right sits the Buddha in plain grey robes, with a small topknot, tight curled hair and long earlobes, with no halo, no glow and no throne, holding a single flower on a short stem at chest height between two fingers. Below left, a small group of seated monks in grey robes face him with blank, still faces; one monk at the front, slightly apart, has his head lifted and a faint smile. The flower is the single muted vermilion accent. Pine branches and mist fill the upper left, with a vast empty lower space. Early autumn stillness. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: the flower held up; the teacher; one smiling monk distinguished from a small, solemn assembly; the flower is the vermilion accent.
- Composition: diagonal from the flower at upper right down to the smiling monk at lower left; mist and empty paper carry the silence between them.
- Iconographic source: Mumonkan 6 (Buddha holds up a flower on Vulture Peak; Mahākāśyapa alone smiles). The scene is a lineage legend and has no fixed painting tradition, so the style follows Muromachi figure-in-landscape work.
- Watch for: halo, golden light, lotus throne, ornate Indian costume, a crowd of dozens of disciples (keep it to five or six), several smiling figures, the flower rendered as a large lotus.

### XII — Facing the Wall

**Prompt.** A tall hanging-scroll composition. A heavy-browed bearded man sits cross-legged in meditation in the lower centre, seen in three-quarter view from behind so that only the edge of his brow and beard shows, facing a blank rough cliff wall of a shallow cave. A red hooded robe covers his head and shoulders and falls around him in broad brushstrokes; this robe is the single muted vermilion accent. The wall is bare grey dry-brush rock with no marks. Above, a few bare branches and a sliver of pale mist; below, a little fallen leaf litter. Cold, silent, patient mood, long years implied by moss at the base of the seat. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: Bodhidharma seated facing a blank wall; the red robe covering the head; heavy brows and beard; the cave or rock wall.
- Composition: figure small and low in the frame, the wall filling most of the scroll as a mass of grey; the viewer looks at his back, which keeps his face unexplained.
- Iconographic source: Daruma paintings of the Muromachi period and later (hooded red robe, large eyes, beard); Shaolin cave tradition, nine years of wall-gazing (Blue Cliff 1 is the exchange with Emperor Wu that precedes it).
- Watch for: a Japanese Daruma doll shape, a pink or cartoon face, a Chinese temple interior in place of a cave, characters or a drawing on the wall, a halo, a second figure, lotus petals under the seat.

### XIII — Pacifying the Mind

**Prompt.** A tall hanging-scroll composition in the manner of Sesshū's Huike painting. On the right, in a shallow rock cave under an overhang, Bodhidharma sits facing the wall with his back to the viewer, wrapped in a red robe that is the single muted vermilion accent. Left of centre, in the open before the cave, the young monk Huike stands in falling snow, snow around his ankles, his head bowed, his left sleeve hanging empty, his right hand holding a small bundle wrapped in plain white cloth forward toward the cave, his posture asking. No wound or blood is shown. Bare twisted pine and sharp cliff lines frame the scene. Cold, quiet night. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: Huike outside in snow; Bodhidharma in the cave, facing away; the offering made by posture and a wrapped bundle, not shown.
- Composition: two figures separated by a gap of empty paper; the cliff leans over the cave; snow suggested by untouched paper and a few flecks of grey wash.
- Iconographic source: Sesshū, Huike Offering His Arm to Bodhidharma (1496): angular cliff, cave at one side, Huike kneeling or standing before Daruma, restrained treatment. Sesshū shows Daruma in profile in a white robe; the red robe here follows card XII for continuity. Mumonkan 41 gives the dialogue (Huike: "Your disciple's mind is not at peace. Please pacify it").
- Watch for: blood, a visible stump or a severed arm, a grimacing face, a roaring fire, a snowy Western pine forest, Bodhidharma turned to face Huike, bright red used on the wound or as spots in the snow. Sources say Huike stood in snow until it reached his waist; the prompt keeps the snow at ankle height for composition, and this may be changed.

### XIV — No Mirror, No Stand

**Prompt.** A tall hanging-scroll composition. A long monastery corridor wall of pale plaster fills the centre, covered with two blocks of abstract brush marks, one neat and even, one looser and larger, both unreadable. At left, a barefoot rice-pounder in a short rough robe with a cloth tied round his head stands looking at the wall, a wooden pounding pole over one shoulder. At right, a robed official with a brush stands beside the wall. A small monk lantern hangs under the eaves. The rice-pounder's sash is the single muted vermilion accent. Tiled roof edge and bamboo at the top, wide empty paper below. Late-night calm. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: the corridor wall with two separate blocks of writing; Huineng as a rice-pounder, plainly dressed and shown as a labourer; a second person writing for him (Zhang Riyong, an official, wrote Huineng's verse at his dictation).
- Composition: wall as a flat central plane; the two text blocks at different scales to suggest two verses; the figure of Huineng at the edge.
- Iconographic source: Platform Sutra, chapter one; Liang Kai's Huineng Chopping Bamboo (Song dynasty): very few quick brush strokes, a simple figure in rough clothes, no ornament. Liang Kai shows Huineng tearing or cutting, so the pose here is the author's.
- Watch for: legible Chinese or pseudo-Chinese characters on the wall (the verses must stay abstract brush marks), a mirror or bodhi tree drawn literally, a golden robe, a grand temple hall, more than three figures, a clean-shaven monk in formal robes in place of the labourer.

### XV — Ordinary Mind

**Prompt.** A tall hanging-scroll composition. On a plain stone path before a small thatched hut, an old teacher in a simple grey robe sits on a low stool at right, calm, his hands resting open on his knees, with a bamboo staff beside him. A younger monk stands at left with palms together, leaning slightly forward, as if asking a question. Between them lies an empty stretch of path and a single tea bowl on a flat stone, its rim touched with vermilion, the single accent. Nearby: a broom and a water bucket. Distant low hills and thin mist above. Unhurried morning. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: Nanquan (older, seated or standing at ease) and Zhaozhou (younger, asking); everyday objects; no grand backdrop.
- Composition: two figures facing each other across empty space at mid height; the humble hut and tools anchor the lower half; the sky stays blank.
- Iconographic source: Mumonkan 19 ("Ordinary mind is the Way"), with the Song and Muromachi tradition of teacher-and-pupil scenes in ordinary settings, such as Hanshan and Shide or Chan master portraits.
- Watch for: Zen-garden raked sand, ornate teahouse, a Japanese tea ceremony, lotus, incense, a heroic pose, a halo, text on the stone. The koan has no fixed props; the tea bowl, broom and bucket are the author's additions and can be dropped.

### XVI — The Wild Fox

**Prompt.** A tall hanging-scroll composition. At the foot of a steep misty mountain, a monks' funeral on a small clearing in the lower half: a few monks in grey robes stand in a half circle around a small pyre, thin smoke rising in a single curl. A small fox body on a wooden board lies before the pyre, wrapped in a monk's robe, with only its pointed ears and tail visible. At left a gaunt old man in a plain robe, drawn faintly in pale grey wash, bows toward the group. The pyre's flame is the single muted vermilion accent. Pines above, empty sky. Solemn dusk. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: a fox; a funeral pyre; monks conducting a rite as for a monk; the old man who was the fox; mountain setting.
- Composition: funeral group low and compact, smoke carrying the eye up to the empty mountain; the old man at the edge, near the mist.
- Iconographic source: Mumonkan 2 (Baizhang's fox): the old man, reborn as a fox for 500 lives, asks to be released, and the fox's body is found behind the mountain and cremated with a monk's rites. Fox imagery in Japanese ink painting is rare; the pose and landscape are the author's.
- Watch for: a fox with nine tails or a fox-spirit woman (Japanese kitsune imagery), a cartoon fox, a luminous ghost, a halo, a large bonfire, a grisly dead animal, fire in several places, gold. The old man may be dropped if the image becomes cluttered.

### XVII — The Shout

**Prompt.** A tall hanging-scroll composition. At right, a stern teacher with a square jaw, heavy brows and an open mouth leans forward from a plain chair, one fist on the knee, shown in bold broad strokes. At left, a young monk is thrown backwards, arms flung up, knees buckling, robe whipped to one side, mouth shut and eyes wide. Between them, a single violent diagonal brush stroke of black ink splashes across the empty paper, with spattered flecks. A vermilion seal-like patch on the teacher's robe is the single muted vermilion accent. Bare ground, a leaning pine at upper left, blank sky. Abrupt, tense, silent. Japanese sumi-e ink-wash painting, Muromachi style after Shūbun and Sesshū, black and grey ink on warm textured cream paper, dry-brush strokes, generous empty space, a single muted vermilion accent, portrait 2:3, narrow plain paper border, no text, no lettering, no gold, no glow, not photorealistic.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: Linji with his mouth open; a monk recoiling; a disturbed, forceful brushstroke between them; no sound effects.
- Composition: strong diagonal from teacher to monk, with empty paper on either side; the splash of ink is the visual shout.
- Iconographic source: Linji Yixuan and the shout (katsu), from the Four Shouts passage of the *Linji lu*. The scene is the author's composition; Muromachi portraits of Chan patriarchs with strong faces and quick, abbreviated robes.
- Watch for: speech bubbles, manga-style speed lines, radiating lines or a glow around the mouth, the Chinese character for the shout (katsu), a stick or a blow (that is Deshan, not Linji), a smiling face, a weeping monk.
