# Pessimistic deck: illustration descriptions for the major cards

Prompts for an image model, one per major card. The card list follows `docs/pessimistic-philosophy-research.md`. Each prompt is self-contained and can be pasted as it stands; the shared style block below is already folded into every prompt.

## Shared visual style

- **Medium:** etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's *Caprichos* and Max Klinger's print cycles. Fine etched line in warm black ink, aquatint grain for the greys, on off-white laid paper.
- **Colour:** hand-tinted with thin, transparent watercolour washes laid over the etched line, as in hand-coloured prints. Four colours only:
  - bone (diluted yellow ochre, about `#D8C59A`);
  - slate (Payne's grey, about `#5E6B78`);
  - caput mortuum (iron-oxide violet-brown, about `#5A2E2E`);
  - verdigris (greyed copper green, about `#6F8C7A`).
  Each card has one dominant wash and at most one other colour in a small area. Most of the paper stays white or carries only aquatint grey.
- **Format:** portrait, 2:3 aspect ratio. The image sits inside a visible rectangular plate mark, the pressed edge of an etching plate, with a margin of untouched paper around it. The plate mark is the same on every card.
- **Figures:** drawn with the weight and gravity of 19th-century printmaking. Faces are observed, not caricatured, except where the source invites satire.
- **Text:** none. No titles, numbers, captions or legible lettering, since image models render them badly; titles are added in layout. Goya's captions are not imitated.
- **Restraint:** no gore, no depiction of suicide or self-harm, no horror-film imagery. Skulls appear only on card V, where vanitas is the subject.
- **Avoid:** photorealism, 3D rendering, digital painting gloss, saturated colour, gothic fantasy styling, comic-book shading.

Dominant wash by card, to keep the four colours in balance across the series:

| Colour | Cards |
| --- | --- |
| Bone | 0, II, V, XIII |
| Slate | III, VII, VIII, XV |
| Caput mortuum | IV, VI, IX, XII |
| Verdigris | I, X, XI, XIV |

Style suffix used at the end of every prompt, with the card's dominant colour named in the prompt itself:

> Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

## Cards

### 0 — The Unborn

**Prompt.** At left, old Silenus, bald, snub-nosed and pot-bellied, with pointed ears and a vine wreath, sits on the ground laughing with his head thrown back, an empty wine cup beside him. At right King Midas, in a Phrygian cap and long robe, recoils with one hand raised and a spear in the other. Behind them stand rose bushes and a low stone spring, with bare paper above. Bone ochre wash dominant, on the ground and robes; a small patch of verdigris on the vine leaves. Uneasy, strange mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: Silenus, old, drunken, laughing; Midas, crowned or capped, recoiling; two figures only; a spring or rose garden as the place of capture.
- Composition: figures in the lower half facing each other, Silenus lower and larger in mass, Midas upright and narrow. Eye line runs from Midas's raised hand to Silenus's open mouth. Upper third left as white paper.
- Iconographic source: Aristotle, Eudemus fr. 6 Ross, quoted in Plutarch, Consolation to Apollonius 115b–e; Nietzsche, Birth of Tragedy §3, where Silenus, forced to speak, breaks into shrill laughter. Plutarch has Midas hunting him; the wine-spring and rose-garden capture comes from Theopompus (via Aelian) and is not in Plutarch. Both are used here as setting only. Satyr-like Silenus follows Greek vase painting and Rubens.
- Watch for: Silenus drawn as a handsome satyr or a goat-legged devil; Midas given golden-touch imagery (gold skin, gold coins), which belongs to a later story; a third figure or a Dionysus; a crown rendered as a modern royal crown; a laughing face turned into a leer that reads as horror.

### I — The Giant Deer

**Prompt.** A single Irish elk (Megaloceros) stands in profile at the centre of a bare winter forest of thin leafless trunks. Its head is lowered almost to the ground under enormous palmate antlers, wider than its body is long. Dry grass and fallen leaves lie at its feet, and faint mist hangs between the trees, with much white paper above. Verdigris wash dominant, on the mist, ground and trunks; a small touch of bone ochre on the animal's flank. Still, heavy, solitary mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: one large deer; antlers visibly oversized and palmate; head bowed; leafless forest; no other animal or person.
- Composition: animal centred slightly below the middle, antlers spreading across most of the plate width and overlapping the trunks behind. Eye line follows the antler beam down to the lowered muzzle. White paper in the upper third.
- Iconographic source: Zapffe, "The Last Messiah" (1933), where deer of an earlier age are said to have died out because their antlers grew too heavy. Zapffe names no species. The Irish elk is the popular candidate and was long cited in this way, although the extinction is no longer attributed to antler size. Palaeontological plates of the nineteenth century are the visual model.
- Watch for: a stag with a modern red-deer rack or a reindeer; antlers with no weight, held upright; a deer lying dead or showing wounds; a hunter, a fire or a skull; a lush or autumn-coloured forest.

### II — The First Illusion, Happiness Here

**Prompt.** Five barefoot children in short tunics play in a walled classical garden. Two chase a hoop along a gravel path, one kneels to place a leaf wreath on a smaller child's head, and one blows a soap bubble that drifts up in the middle of the plate. A marble fountain basin with a thin hairline crack stands at left, a stone herm and a cypress at right. Warm low light, long shadows. Bone ochre wash dominant, on the ground, tunics and stone; a small touch of verdigris on the cypress. Light, open mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: several children at play; classical setting (columns or herm, fountain, cypress); one quiet sign of fragility, here the soap bubble and a hairline crack in the basin.
- Composition: children grouped in the lower two-thirds, the bubble at the optical centre against bare paper, the cypress and herm framing the right edge. Eye line runs from the hoop to the wreath to the bubble.
- Iconographic source: Eduard von Hartmann, Philosophie des Unbewussten (1869), first stage of illusion, happiness in this life, matched to antiquity and childhood. The bubble echoes the homo bulla motif of vanitas art; the hairline crack is kept small so that the scene reads as pleasant first.
- Watch for: a cracked column or ruin that dominates the scene; adults or a skull added; children drawn in modern clothes or as cherubs with wings; a gloomy sky; the crack drawn so large that it announces the point.

### III — The Second Illusion, Happiness Hereafter

**Prompt.** In the lower third, a group of medieval pilgrims in hooded cloaks and broad hats, carrying staffs and wearing scallop shells, stand on a rocky path, seen mostly from behind, their faces and raised hands turned up. High above, a walled city with towers, spires and a gate rests on banks of cloud. Its lower walls and towers are fading into thin cloud, so that the sky shows through gaps in the stonework. Slate wash dominant, on the sky and cloaks; a small touch of bone ochre on the highest towers. Awed, quiet mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: pilgrims with staffs and shells; a celestial city on clouds; the city visibly thinning, with clouds showing through its walls.
- Composition: figures small at the bottom, city filling the upper half. Eye line rises from the pilgrims' hands through the gate to the highest spire. The thinning is strongest toward the left of the city and along its base.
- Iconographic source: Hartmann, Philosophie des Unbewussten, second stage, happiness in a transcendent life, matched to the Middle Ages and youth. The city follows the Heavenly Jerusalem of medieval illumination and of Dürer's Apocalypse woodcuts, with the Klinger habit of a small figure under a large vision.
- Watch for: a solid fortress with no thinning; the city drawn as a ruin, which gives a different meaning; angels, rays of light or a halo; pilgrims kneeling in prayer in fantasy robes; modern hikers; the whole sky turned dark and stormy.

### IV — The Third Illusion, Progress

**Prompt.** A procession of about fifteen nineteenth-century figures in frock coats, top hats, crinolines and bonnets walks along a straight road across a flat plain, led by a man carrying a blank pennant, seen from behind and slightly to one side. Ahead, a horizon of factory chimneys, smoke and railway embankments with converging tracks stretches into the distance, each row fainter and farther off than the last. A small train crosses far away. Caput mortuum wash dominant, on the smoke, coats and road; a small touch of bone ochre on the sky at the horizon. Earnest, steady mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: a crowd walking one way; period dress; chimneys and railway lines on the horizon; the horizon visibly farther than it should be.
- Composition: procession in the lower half, running diagonally into depth; rails and chimneys diminish toward a vanishing point set high in the plate. The aquatint grain thickens toward the horizon so that the destination loses definition. White paper above the smoke.
- Iconographic source: Hartmann, Philosophie des Unbewussten, third stage, happiness in the future of the world-process, matched to modernity and adulthood. The visual model is nineteenth-century industrial prints and Klinger's processions, in which a crowd moves as one body.
- Watch for: a plain procession with no sense of receding; chimneys as a dark wall that is close; a pennant or banner carrying pseudo-text; modern clothes or hi-vis; a train rushing at the viewer; people drawn as a mocking crowd, since the satire is mild.

### V — Vanity

**Prompt.** A Dutch vanitas still life on a bare wooden table, viewed from the front. At the centre a human skull rests beside an hourglass. A snuffed candle in a brass holder sends up a thin thread of smoke. Tools of labour lie around them: a spade, a hammer, a pair of compasses and a closed ledger. At upper right, a window stands open on a low, very bright sun, left as bare paper with fine rays. Bone ochre wash dominant, on the skull, table and wall; a small touch of slate in the window shadow. Quiet, orderly mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: one skull; hourglass; snuffed candle with smoke; tools of labour (spade, hammer, compasses, ledger); a window to a bright sun. This is the only card with a skull.
- Composition: still life on the lower half of the plate, the window at upper right as the brightest spot, the unpainted sun a disc of white paper. The eye runs from the sun along the shadow of the hourglass to the skull.
- Iconographic source: Ecclesiastes 1:2–3 and 2:11 (hevel, "vanity of vanities", "what profit hath a man of all his labour which he taketh under the sun"). The vanitas still life of Leiden and Haarlem, c. 1620–1660, supplies the objects.
- Watch for: legible text on the ledger or on a scroll; a second or third skull; a lit candle; fruit, flowers or coins, which belong to other vanitas types; a coloured or glowing sun; a gloomy, gothic room.

### VI — The Pendulum

**Prompt.** An allegory on a bare plain beneath a plain sky. A great iron pendulum hangs from the top edge of the plate on a long rod, with a heavy disc at the end; a faint arc of dashed lines on the ground marks its swing. At the left end of the arc, a draped figure kneels doubled over, face twisted in pain, one hand pressed to the side. At the right end, a slouching man sits on a stone block, arms stretched overhead, mouth open in a wide yawn, a closed book at his feet. Caput mortuum wash dominant, on the pendulum, ground and sky; a small touch of slate in the yawning man's coat. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: a large pendulum; a figure in pain at one extreme and a yawning, idle figure at the other; the arc of the swing indicated.
- Composition: pendulum rod on the central vertical, figures in the lower corners, the weight at mid-height. A symmetrical scheme with the two figures balancing each other. The eye line swings along the arc between the faces.
- Iconographic source: Schopenhauer, Die Welt als Wille und Vorstellung I §57, life swinging "like a pendulum" between pain and boredom. The symmetrical allegory and the nude or draped figures under a heavy apparatus follow Klinger's cycles (Vom Tode, Ein Leben). Klinger drew no such pendulum; the combination is new.
- Watch for: a grandfather clock or clock face; a pendulum drawn as a mere ornament, with no weight; the figure in pain looking violent, bloody or self-harming (keep it to a doubled-over posture); the yawner drawn asleep or dead; both figures given the same expression.

### VII — Nature and the Icelander

**Prompt.** A small traveller in a heavy long coat and cap, with a staff, stands at lower left on a barren sunlit plain, seen in three-quarter view from behind, looking up. Before him sits a colossal woman, her torso upright, her back and elbow resting against a mountain so that she seems made of the same mass. Her face is between beautiful and terrible, with very black eyes and black hair, and she stares down at him. Slate wash dominant, on the mountain, her robe and the sky; a small touch of bone ochre on the plain. Silent, severe mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: tiny traveller; colossal woman seated on the ground, torso upright, back and elbow against a mountain; face between beautiful and terrible; very black eyes and hair; her fixed gaze on him; arid ground.
- Composition: the woman fills the upper two-thirds, her face near the plate's upper centre; the traveller is a small dark mark in the lower left, giving a scale ratio of about one to twenty. Eye line runs from the traveller's raised face up to her eyes.
- Iconographic source: Leopardi, "Dialogo della Natura e di un Islandese" (Operette morali, 1824): "una forma smisurata di donna seduta in terra, col busto ritto, appoggiato il dosso e il gomito a una montagna, ... di volto mezzo tra bello e terribile, di occhi e di capelli nerissimi", alive and not carved. The Icelander meets her while crossing the interior of Africa under the equator. The text gives no clothing, so the heavy coat marks him as a northerner. Colossal personifications in Klinger and Goya's aquatint The Colossus (c. 1814–18) are the print models; the painting of the same subject in the Prado is no longer firmly attributed to Goya.
- Watch for: a gentle mother-goddess or Gaia with flowers; a woman who looks like stone, since the text says she is alive; the mountain drawn separately from her; a traveller of normal scale; lions, tents, camels or palm trees; a black skin tone assigned to Nature, which the text does not state; blank or blind eyes.

### VIII — The Porcupines

**Prompt.** A group of nine porcupines on open snow under a low winter sky. Seven sit in a loose ring with a small gap between each animal, their quills lowered and relaxed. Near the centre, two porcupines sit too close together, quills raised and crossed, one flinching back from the other. Bare snow takes up most of the plate, with a line of leafless trees far behind and a faint track of footprints between the animals. Slate wash dominant, on the sky and the shadows on the snow; a small touch of bone ochre on the animals' fur. Cold, still mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: porcupines with long quills; snow and winter; most animals at a settled moderate distance; one pair still too close, quills raised.
- Composition: the ring in the lower half, seen from slightly above so the spacing reads clearly; the crowded pair at the optical centre. Upper half left as slate sky over white snow.
- Iconographic source: Schopenhauer, *Parerga and Paralipomena* II (1851), the parable of the porcupines that huddle for warmth, prick one another, and settle at the distance Schopenhauer calls politeness. The section number (§396) is unconfirmed. Animal plates in 19th-century natural history are the model for the animals.
- Watch for: hedgehogs in place of porcupines (the German is *Stachelschweine*); cartoon or cute animals; blood or injury from the quills; a forest so dense that the snowfield disappears; human figures.

### IX — The Shattered God

**Prompt.** A vast pale sphere, larger than the plate can hold, floats in the upper half against a dark aquatint sky. It has split along many fine cracks, and its fragments drift outward and downward. Near the sphere the fragments are smooth shards; farther away they become stars, then stones, a tree, a fish, a bird and a small walking figure, scattered across a dim landscape in the lower third. The sphere itself is featureless, with no face. Caput mortuum wash dominant, in the dark sky around the sphere; a small touch of bone ochre on the sphere and the nearest shards. Quiet, slow mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: one unified form breaking apart; fragments that turn into the many things of the world (stars, stones, plants, animals, a person); a sense of slow dispersal.
- Composition: sphere cropped by the top edge of the plate; fragments fanning down in a cone toward the lower corners. The eye follows the cracks outward, then down to the small figure.
- Iconographic source: Philipp Mainländer, *Die Philosophie der Erlösung* (1876): a primal unity breaks into the multiplicity of the world, and the world's history is the course of that unity's dying. No print tradition illustrates Mainländer; the cosmological plates of Redon's *Noirs* and Klinger's *Brahms Fantasy* are the nearest models.
- Watch for: a human or bearded God figure; a crucifix or any Christian image; an explosion with fire and light rays; a planet with continents; glowing edges on the cracks.

### X — The Insomniac

**Prompt.** A man in shirtsleeves sits upright on a plain wooden chair beside a tall open window at night, his elbow on the sill and his eyes wide open, looking out. A small oil lamp burns on a table beside him next to a few closed books. Below the window a city lies asleep: dark roofs, chimneys and one empty street with a single dim lamp-post. The sky above is clear and still, with a few small stars. Verdigris wash dominant, on the night sky and the roofs; a small touch of bone ochre in the lamplight on his face and the table. Calm, wakeful mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: one man, awake, eyes open; night; a lamp; a sleeping city seen below the window.
- Composition: the man in the left half, in profile or three-quarter view, the window filling the right half. The lamp is the only warm light. The composition inverts Goya's Capricho 43, where the figure sleeps at a desk and the creatures of the night are awake.
- Iconographic source: Emil Cioran, *Pe culmile disperării* (1934) and later essays on his insomnia, which began in his early twenties. Goya, *El sueño de la razón produce monstruos* (Capricho 43, 1799), as the inverted model.
- Watch for: owls, bats or other creatures from Goya's plate; the man slumped, asleep or with his head in his hands; a modern city with electric light; a moon so bright that it competes with the lamp; a dramatic or haunted expression.

### XI — The Stone

**Prompt.** A steep, barren mountain slope fills the plate from upper right to lower left. Near the top, a strongly built man in a short tunic walks down the slope with a steady, measured step, his arms loose at his sides and his face composed. Far below, at the foot of the mountain, a large round boulder has come to rest on level ground. The path he climbed is visible as a worn track. Verdigris wash dominant, on the slope, moss on the rocks and the boulder; a small touch of slate in the shadows of the sky. Steady, unhurried mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: Sisyphus walking down, without the stone; the boulder at rest at the bottom; the long slope between them.
- Composition: strong diagonal from the figure near the top to the boulder at the bottom; the figure small, the slope large. The eye travels down the track with him.
- Iconographic source: Albert Camus, *Le Mythe de Sisyphe* (1942), which concentrates on the descent, when Sisyphus returns toward the stone and is conscious of his fate. The usual image, Sisyphus pushing the stone uphill (Titian, Stuck), is avoided on purpose.
- Watch for: Sisyphus pushing or carrying the boulder, which is the conventional image and the wrong moment; a tormented or straining face; chains or a Hades setting with flames; a smiling face, which overstates Camus's "one must imagine Sisyphus happy".

### XII — Compassion

Caput mortuum wash dominant, on the woman's dress, the road and the sky at dusk; a small touch of bone ochre on the blanket and the bowl. Quiet, attentive mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: a person tending a stranger; an animal also being cared for; an ordinary setting; no visible wounds beyond a bandage.
- Composition: the two groups side by side in the lower half, the woman and the boy echoing each other's posture. The empty road leads the eye away into white paper.
- Iconographic source: Schopenhauer, *Über die Grundlage der Moral* (1840), where compassion (*Mitleid*) is the only moral incentive and extends to animals, and *Die Welt als Wille und Vorstellung* I §67. The Good Samaritan tradition in prints supplies the composition, without its religious figures.
- Watch for: blood, injuries or a figure who looks dead; a nurse's uniform or a hospital; halos or religious dress; sentimental tears; the dog drawn as a cartoon.

### XIII — The Broom

**Prompt.** A slope of black, cracked lava fills the lower two-thirds of the plate. Clumps of broom grow out of the cracks, their thin stems covered in small yellow flowers. Above, the cone of Vesuvius rises at upper right with a thin plume of smoke. Far below at left, the broken walls and columns of a ruined Roman town lie half buried. There are no people. Bone ochre wash dominant, on the broom flowers and the pale sky; a small touch of slate in the smoke. Sparse, open mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: broom (Spanish broom, *Spartium junceum*) in yellow flower; lava ground; Vesuvius smoking; ruins below.
- Composition: broom in the foreground at large scale, the volcano and ruins small behind. The yellow flowers are the brightest colour on the plate.
- Iconographic source: Giacomo Leopardi, *La ginestra, o il fiore del deserto* (1836, published 1845), written near Torre del Greco: the broom grows on the lava of Vesuvius above buried towns. Grand Tour views of Vesuvius are the landscape model.
- Watch for: an eruption with fire and lava flows; lush meadow flowers or gorse; a golden glow; tourists or figures in the ruins; the volcano filling the plate and crowding out the plant.

### XIV — The Stilled Wheel

**Prompt.** A large wooden spoked wheel, taller than a man, stands upright and motionless in long grass at left, slightly tilted, with ivy growing over its rim. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: a large wheel at rest; a seated figure in contemplation; a wide, quiet landscape.
- Composition: wheel and figure in the lower half, balancing each other; the view occupies the right and upper parts of the plate as open white paper. The figure's back is to the wheel.
- Iconographic source: Schopenhauer, *Die Welt als Wille und Vorstellung* I §38, where in aesthetic contemplation "the wheel of Ixion stands still", and §68 on the denial of the will. The figure seen from behind before a view follows Caspar David Friedrich.
- Watch for: a figure bound to the wheel (Ixion's punishment), which is the opposite of the scene; flames on the wheel; a wagon or a mill; a dramatic sky; a lotus pose or Buddhist imagery.

### XV — The Last Messiah

**Prompt.** On a low bare rise at left, a lone man in a plain coat stands with one hand open toward a crowd below him. The crowd of thirty or so men, women and children stands on the plain at right; most have turned their backs to him and are walking away, and a few look back over their shoulders. Beyond them, the plain runs out into an empty, quiet landscape with no buildings, under a wide pale sky. Slate wash dominant, on the sky and the distant plain; a small touch of caput mortuum on the speaker's coat. Hushed, final mood. Etching with aquatint in the manner of Goya's Caprichos and Max Klinger, fine warm black etched line and aquatint grain on off-white laid paper, hand-tinted with thin transparent watercolour washes limited to bone ochre, slate grey, caput mortuum violet-brown and greyed verdigris, mostly unpainted paper, visible rectangular plate mark with a wide paper margin, portrait 2:3, no text, no lettering, no gore, not photorealistic, not digital.

**Notes.**

- Required elements: one speaker; a crowd mostly turning away; an empty landscape beyond; no violence.
- Composition: the speaker small and high at left, the crowd in the middle ground at right, the empty land and sky taking up the upper half. The eye moves from his open hand across the backs of the crowd to the empty horizon, which leads back to card 0.
- Iconographic source: Peter Wessel Zapffe, "Den sidste Messias" (1933), whose Messiah tells humanity to be infertile and let the earth be silent after them. In the essay the crowd then turns on him; the card shows the moment before. Klinger's crowd scenes and Goya's *Disparates* are the print models.
- Watch for: a Christ-like figure with halo, robe or cross; a mob attacking him; a ruined or post-apocalyptic city; corpses or skulls; a preacher's pulpit; a dramatic sunset.
